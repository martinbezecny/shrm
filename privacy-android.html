Simple Heart Rate Monitor

Privacy policy · Android version 3.2

Nothing leavesyour phone.

This is short because there is almost nothing to describe.

In one sentence

Simple Heart Rate Monitor collects no data, holds no internet permission, and has no way to send anything anywhere.

What the app collects

Nothing. There is no account, no sign-in, no registration, and no profile. The app asks for no name, email address, phone number, or any other identifier.

The one thing you can tell it about yourself is your age and sex, and only if you ask it to estimate your maximum heart rate for the zone display. That is optional, stays on the phone, and is described under Heart rate zones below.

It contains no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no third-party software development kits. It makes no network requests of any kind, because it does not request the INTERNET permission: Android itself will not let the app open a connection, whatever its code might attempt.

Because nothing is collected, there are no third parties to disclose. No advertising network, analytics provider, cloud service, support platform, or other partner receives any information about you or your use of the app. In the Google Play Data safety section the app declares that no data is collected and none is shared.

Your heart rate readings

Readings arrive from your Bluetooth sensor, appear on screen, and are written to files inside the app's own storage on your device. They stay there. They are never transmitted, never uploaded, and never seen by the developer or anyone else. The developer has no access to your readings, your session history, or your settings, and no technical means of obtaining them.

Session history is likewise stored only on the device, as plain CSV files in the app's private directory, which no other app can read.

The live graph added in version 3.2 draws the last five minutes from a buffer held in memory while the app runs. It writes nothing beyond the session file already described, and the buffer is emptied when recording stops or the app closes.

Heart rate zones

Version 3.0 added five training zones, derived from your maximum heart rate. They stay off until you switch them on. You set the maximum in one of two ways: type it in yourself, or let the app estimate it from your age and sex. Type it in and the app never asks for either.

If you do choose the estimate, the two figures feed one arithmetic formula on your own phone and are used for nothing else. They are written to a private settings file of their own, ZonePrefs, kept apart from the rest of your preferences so that the backup rules can exclude it by name. They are never transmitted, never used to profile you, and the developer has no way of seeing them. Change or clear them at any time in the same dialog, or switch back to entering a maximum directly.

When several consistent readings exceed your current maximum, the app raises it automatically. That measured value is kept in the same private file and goes nowhere either.

Health Connect and Google Fit

The app does not use Health Connect, Google Fit, or any other health platform. It neither reads from nor writes to them, and it requests no body sensor or activity recognition permission. Should a later version add Health Connect support, it will require your explicit permission, and this policy will be updated before that version is released.

Exporting and sharing

You can export a session as a CSV or PDF file, or every session at once as a ZIP archive. The app prepares the file on your device and hands it to the standard Android share sheet, at which point you choose where it goes: Files, Drive, Gmail, a messaging app, or anywhere else installed on your phone. The file is passed as a temporary content URI, which grants the app you picked access to that one file and to nothing else.

This is the only route by which your data can deliberately leave the device, and only you can open it. Once a file has been sent somewhere, it is governed by the privacy policy of whatever service or app you sent it to, not by this one. Choose deliberately.

An exported CSV carries the zone thresholds that were in force for that session, meaning the maximum heart rate and the five boundaries calculated from it. Your age and sex are not written to it, nor to the PDF, which shows the same zone lines you see on screen.

Files prepared for export are held temporarily in the app's cache and removed automatically within a day.

Importing works the same way in reverse. The app reads only the files you hand it through the system file picker; it requests no storage permission and cannot see the rest of your phone.

Android backup

Android's own backup service is left switched on, but the health data is withheld from it. Your recorded sessions and your zone settings, age, sex, and the manual or measured maximum, are excluded from the daily backup into your Google account, on every Android version the app supports. Theme, text size, voice readout, and donation state are ordinary preference data and still restore after a reinstall.

On Android 12 and later, a direct phone-to-phone transfer is treated differently: it is local, you start it yourself when setting up the new device, and it carries everything, history included, so that replacing a phone is not a data-loss event. To move your history at any other time, use the ZIP export and import on the history screen.

If you would rather no backup happened at all, turn it off for this app in your phone's system settings, under Google, then Backup.

Deleting your data

Delete sessions individually, or several at once, from the history screen inside the app. To remove everything, uninstall the app: that erases all recordings and settings from the device. Export anything you want to keep before you do, because deletion is permanent and there is no backup held anywhere else.

Settings you enter

Your text size, theme, spoken-reading, live-graph, and zone-line preferences are stored on the device so the app remembers them. Alongside them the app keeps a count of how often it has been opened and whether you have dismissed the support prompt, purely so that the prompt does not return too soon. All of it lives in the app's private settings file, is used for nothing but the features it configures, and leaves the phone only through the Android backup described above.

Spoken readings

When voice readout is switched on, the app hands the current number to the text-to-speech engine built into your phone, usually Google's and sometimes the manufacturer's. It passes the reading and nothing else: no identifier, no session, no history. What that engine does with the words it is given is governed by its own privacy policy and by your phone's speech settings, both outside this app's control. Leave voice readout off and it is never invoked at all.

Permissions

On Android 12 and later the app requests one thing: Nearby devices, meaning Bluetooth scanning and connection. It is used solely to discover and communicate with your heart rate sensor. The scan is declared with Android's neverForLocation flag, which tells the system that the app must not derive your location from the devices it finds.

On Android 11 and older the system delivers Bluetooth scan results only to apps holding a location permission, and only while Location is switched on. The app therefore asks for it on those versions, and will tell you if Location is off. It does not read your location, does not store it, and does not use it for anything: the permission exists solely because older versions of Android make it the price of a Bluetooth scan.

Since version 3.1 the app keeps recording with the screen off or another app in front, through a foreground service that shows a persistent notification with the current heart rate and a Stop button. On Android 13 and later it asks for permission to post that notification. Refusing it hides the notification but does not stop recording. The notification is drawn by Android on your own phone; depending on your lock screen settings, your heart rate may be visible there while a session runs.

The app also declares the foreground service and wake lock permissions, which Android grants without asking. They keep the recording and its reconnection attempts alive while the screen is off and give the app no access to any data.

No camera, microphone, contacts, photos, storage, body sensor, or internet permission is requested.

Supporting the developer

After the app has been opened a number of times, it may ask once whether you would like to support its development. Tapping the button hands a Buy Me a Coffee address to your browser, and the app's involvement ends there: it attaches nothing about you to that address and is never told what you did next. Your answer is recorded on the device only so that the prompt knows to wait, or to stop asking for good.

If you do choose to donate, that transaction is between you and Buy Me a Coffee, under their privacy policy rather than this one. Dismiss the prompt and nothing leaves the phone.

Google

Your download is a transaction with Google, not with the developer. Google Play handles distribution and any payment, may collect information about the download and about your use of the Play Store, and may provide the developer with aggregate statistics and crash reports that do not identify individual users. That processing is governed by Google's privacy policy, not this one.

This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no scripts, loads no fonts or images from other domains, and carries no analytics. It is hosted on GitHub, which records server logs, including visitors' IP addresses, in the ordinary course of serving the pages.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone, of any age.

Your rights

Rights of access, correction, erasure, and portability under the GDPR apply to personal data held by a controller. No personal data is collected by, transmitted to, or held by the developer, so there is nothing held about you to access, correct, or erase.

Your recordings are already in your own hands: on your own device, in an open text format you can read, export, or destroy at will.

Changes

If a future version of the app ever collects or transmits anything, this policy and the app's Google Play Data safety disclosures will both be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described in the release notes. Any such feature would be optional and off by default.
